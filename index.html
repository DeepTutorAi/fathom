A descent in four zones

Fathom

Beneath the surface lies the largest living space on Earth. Most of it has never been seen.

I · Epipelagic · 0–200 m

The sunlit sea

Nearly all of the ocean's photosynthesis happens here, in one thin layer of light.

II · Mesopelagic · 200–1,000 m

The twilight zone

Light fades to a rumour of blue. Too dim to feed plants, just bright enough to hunt by.

Diel vertical migration

The great migration

Each night, billions of animals rise from these depths to feed near the surface, then sink again before dawn. It is the largest migration on Earth.

III · Bathypelagic · 1,000–4,000 m

The midnight zone

No sunlight reaches this far. The only light is the light that life makes.

Bioluminescence

Move slowly.

Most animals here make their own light. Disturb the water and it answers.

Hover or tap the lights

IV · Abyssopelagic · 4,000 m +

The abyss

Near-freezing water. Four hundred times the pressure of the surface. And far below, a light that belongs to nothing alive.

Unidentified structure · 4,800 m

Something has been waiting.

We have better maps of the surface of Mars than of our own sea floor.
